    THE RULES

    Chess, with consequences.

    Start with ordinary chess. Add remorse and a little forgiveness.

    1. Taking a piece changes you. Every capture marks the capturing piece with an orange remorse ring. It may move to empty squares and block attacks, but it cannot capture or give check.
    2. Move a bishop to grant absolution. After your bishop moves, all remorseful allies on the eight neighbouring squares are forgiven. Their rings disappear and they may capture again.
    3. Protect your king. Check, checkmate, castling, en passant and promotion still apply, with threats calculated using remorse. Kings are never captured and must remain apart.
    Details used in this version
    • The bishop must move. Moving a guilty piece beside a stationary bishop does not cure it.
    • Bishops feel remorse too. They can still absolve others, but cannot absolve themselves.
    • Kings feel remorse too, but must always stay on non-adjacent squares.
    • Remorse stays with a promoted pawn. Promoting to a bishop does not absolve neighbours on that move.
    • Stalemate, threefold repetition, 50 moves without a pawn move or capture, and bare kings end in a draw. Other material draws are not assumed.
    • Undo takes back your whole turn against the computer, or one move in a two-player game.

    These edge rulings make the short variant description playable; they are this implementation’s interpretation.
